Fiqh

Some Rules and Regulations About Fasting

Fasting in Ramadan is obligatory on every sane, adult Muslim, male or female. It was first required of Muslims during the second year of Hijrah. The way to fast is to abstain from eating, drinking, and intimate marital relations from dawn to sunset. Children over eight years are to be encouraged to fast gradually so that they get used to fasting. One must have the intention (niyah) during the night to fast the following day of Ramadan; it is sufficient to have the intention to fast during Ramadan at the beginning of the month.

Ramadan has a greater meaning than fasting alone. It involves a moral and social code, and a commitment to charitable deeds. Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) said that whoever fasts but indulges in falsehood, let him or her know that giving up food and drink alone shall not benefit them in front of Allah. Ramadan is therefore a period of meditation and prayer during which Muslims tend to withdraw a little from their usual daily affairs and spend long periods in the mosque, in addition to the five daily prayers observed throughout the year.

Although fasting during Ramadan is obligatory, under certain circumstances a fast may be postponed:

  • During sickness
  • If traveling more than 60 miles
  • Women who are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • During their monthly period, women are not only exempted but also forbidden to fast; the same applies during post-natal discharge

All days missed for any of the above reasons are to be made up when the cause of exemption is over, on any day except the days of Eid (fasting on Eid days is forbidden). Full exemption from fasting is granted by Allah to those permanently incapacitated by old age, an incurable disease, or uninterrupted hard labor; they are required to feed a poor person for each day they cannot fast, if they can afford it, or pay the cost of the food (fidyah) in charity instead.

Fasting is invalidated if a person, while fasting, deliberately eats, drinks, vomits, or lets an object pass through the throat; a fast invalidated this way should be made up after Ramadan. The Kaffarah (atonement) for breaking the fast through intimate marital relations is more serious: fasting sixty consecutive days, or if unable, feeding sixty poor people. Fasting remains valid if the fast is broken through compulsion or genuine forgetting.

While fasting, it is permissible to use eye drops, wear contact lenses, receive injections, have a blood test, shower, swim (though it is better to avoid diving, which may let water into the throat), use miswak or brush with toothpaste, rinse the mouth or nostrils with water without swallowing it, and apply perfume or cosmetics.

Zakat al-Fitr, Fidyah, and Zakat al-Maal

Muslims are urged to be charitable always, but especially so in Ramadan. Prophet Muhammad (PBUH) used to give away much during this month.

Zakat al-Fitr (Fitrana):

A special mandatory charity, paid by the head of the family on behalf of every dependent member, during Ramadan or before the Eid al-Fitr prayers. Dependents include all children, even one born during Ramadan before the Eid prayers, and any helpers or parents financially supported by the head of the family. A companion of the Prophet (PBUH), Abu Sa'eed al-Khudree, said the Prophet's companions used to give it as a Sa'a of food, dried dates, barley, raisins, or dried cheese (Bukhari, Arabic/English vol. 2, p. 340, no. 582); most scholars put one Sa'a at roughly 2.6 to 3 kg. Zakat al-Fitr is estimated this year at $— per person, though one may give more.

Fidyah:

A donation of money or food made when someone cannot fast for the required number of days and will not be able to make them up. Fidyah is equal to the cost of a full meal, paid for each missed fast; it is estimated this year at $— per missed fast, or your own typical meal cost if different.

Zakat al-Maal:

One of the five pillars of Islam, alongside prayer, fasting, Hajj, and belief in Allah (SWT) and His Messenger. Every sane, adult Muslim who owns wealth over the nisab threshold must pay 2.5% of that wealth as zakat. The Qur'an (2:110) says: “And establish prayer and give zakat, and whatever good you put forward for yourselves, you will find it with Allah; surely Allah sees what you do.” Many Muslims pay Zakat al-Maal on their savings during Ramadan, for a period of one full year.
